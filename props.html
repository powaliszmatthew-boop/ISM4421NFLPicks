How it works

  1. Data. Every QB, RB, WR, and TE game from last season and this one, from the open nflverse weekly player stats. It's downloaded fresh, so new weeks show up automatically.
  2. Projection. For each player and stat, a weighted average of their games (recent games count most, last season counts half), pulled slightly toward the position average. It's nudged for the opponent's defense against that position and for the team's implied points from the betting line.
  3. Learning week to week. The model walks through every week in order. It projects that week, sees the results, and learns. It corrects projections that run high or low for each kind of player, how spread out results are, and how often results land above or below each line. Each new week of games updates all three.
  4. Probability. The projection becomes a full range of outcomes (a gamma curve for yards, a normal curve for QB passing volume, and count distributions for catches, carries, and touchdowns). That range gives the chance of going over or under any line, including pushes on whole-number lines.
  5. Edge. Same rule as the game picks: expected value = win chance × payout − loss chance. A side is suggested when that's at least your minimum edge.

How accurate is it?

Tested walk-forward on the 2025 season and the first six weeks of 2026, where every projection used only earlier games. Projections were about 2–4% more accurate than a player's simple last-8-game average. When the model said 45% or 55% at a line, the actual rate was within about 3 points. It's well calibrated, not magic: sportsbooks set prop lines with similar information, so real edges are usually small and rare.

For education and entertainment. Prop lines are set by professionals and include the book's margin. Injuries, weather, and role changes the stats don't show yet can make a projection wrong. Never bet more than you can afford to lose. Need help? Call 1-800-GAMBLER.
